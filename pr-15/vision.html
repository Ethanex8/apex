System Architecture and Contracts ​

Apex is a full-stack hardware and robotics project that builds every layer of a compact, indoor-safe (about 2.5 kg) autonomous quadruped from first principles. Across six integrated subsystems, we design a custom 32-bit processor on programmable logic, a 6-layer circuit board, a C compiler, a real-time kernel, force-controlled brushless actuators, a solid-state battery protection spine, and a synchronized stereo-vision head. Each mathematical derivation, circuit layout, compiler pass, and control loop is developed directly alongside the subsystem that requires it, unifying software, electronics, and rigid-body dynamics around one physical machine.

1. Six Integrated Subsystems ​

Every subsystem of Apex functions first as a verifiable standalone desktop build and then bolts directly into the final quadruped without circuit-board redesigns or throwaway prototypes. Those six layers progress bottom-up from custom digital logic and motor power electronics to whole-body locomotion and stereo navigation:

  1. Custom Processor and Spine (brain): We design a 32-bit processor implementing the open Reduced Instruction Set Computer, Fifth Generation (RISC-V) instruction set in the SystemVerilog hardware-description language and compile it onto a reprogrammable logic chip (Field-Programmable Gate Array, or FPGA). That processor mounts onto a custom 6-layer Printed Circuit Board (PCB) with 32 MB of Random-Access Memory (RAM) and executes two-lane 16-bit neural dot products via a custom multiply-accumulate instruction (mac). Above the hardware, brain runs our custom SysC C-subset compiler and a deterministic preemptive task scheduler (Real-Time Operating System, or RTOS).
  2. Force-Controlled Brushless Actuators (drive): Flat pancake brushless motors pair with a custom 24 V-rated three-phase DC-to-AC motor switching board (inverter) and an 8:1 low-backlash planetary gearbox designed in 3D Computer-Aided Design (CAD). By regulating motor coil current and output torque at 20,000 Hz via Field-Oriented Control (FOC), each actuator moves easily by hand when compliant or unpowered (backdrivable) and behaves as a finger-safe (under 2.5 N·m) programmable spring and damper.
  3. Battery Spine and Protection (power): A heavy-copper power distribution board delivers a touch-safe 19.2 V nominal (21.9 V max) motor bus from a six-cell series lithium-iron-phosphate pack (6S LiFePO4) alongside a regulated 5 V, 4 A logic rail. It ramps bus voltage smoothly on startup via a soft pre-charge limiter and isolates short circuits in under 5 µs using a bidirectional solid-state circuit breaker (electronic fuse, or e-fuse) interlocked with a physical mushroom emergency-stop (E-stop) switch. A recursive nonlinear state estimator (Extended Kalman Filter, or EKF) tracks remaining battery state of charge online under pulsed motor loads.
  4. Articulated Hopping Leg (limb): Before scaling to twelve joints, three drive actuators bolt together into a single three-joint (3-Degree-of-Freedom, or 3-DOF) leg on a desktop vertical rail to master gravity compensation, impact absorption, and cyclic hopping. Once verified on the stand, that exact hardware assembly transfers directly onto the final quadruped chassis as Front-Left Leg 1.
  5. Dynamic Quadruped Platform (walker): Four limb assemblies, the power spine, and the brain computer mount into a lightweight carbon-fiber and machined-aluminum torso bay. A model-based predictive balance controller and a compact scaled-integer (quantized) neural locomotion policy trained in the MuJoCo 3D physics simulator run natively on our custom RISC-V processor.
  6. Stereo Vision and Navigation (sight): Two global-shutter cameras phase-locked to brain's hardware shutter pulse measure 3D room depth and fuse visual features with motion-sensor data (Visual-Inertial Odometry, or VIO). On top of that odometry stream, sight builds live 3D obstacle maps (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping, or SLAM) and sends bounded velocity commands down to brain.

Each subsystem graduates from its own desktop verification rig before integrating into the next mechanical or electrical assembly:

2. Two-Compute Partition and Interface Contracts ​

Autonomous legged robots run two workloads with fundamentally different timing requirements: joint torque and balance loops must meet strict millisecond deadlines on every tick, whereas stereo depth mapping and path planning process megabytes of image frames at variable latency. Apex partitions those workloads cleanly between brain in the torso and sight in the camera head across a hardware-synchronized serial bridge:

  1. Deterministic Real-Time Spinal Cord (brain, 50 to 100 MHz FPGA single-chip computer, or System-on-Chip (SoC), combining processor, memory, and peripheral controllers):
    • Three-layer quadruped locomotion networks (with layer neuron widths 48→128→128→12 evaluated at 50 Hz) require 24,064 Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operations per 20 ms policy tick to update target joint angles for the 1,000 Hz joint controllers.
    • Adding a custom packed two-lane 16-bit mac instruction in RISC-V's reserved custom-0 operation-code slot and teaching SysC to emit it lets a 50 MHz processor built from configurable FPGA logic cells complete the forward pass in under 0.6 ms, leaving over 96% processor headroom.
    • Those remaining cycles service the 1,000 Hz 6-axis Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU, ICM-42688-P accelerometer and gyroscope), dual 5 Mbps differential motor buses, safety limits, and telemetry.
    • Mounted at the center of the torso with fixed static arrays—requiring zero garbage collection or dynamic memory allocation—brain isolates its sub-2 µs, 1,000 Hz control loop behind a 150 ms zero-velocity watchdog timeout whenever sight goes silent.
  2. High-Bandwidth Visual Cortex (sight, Companion Linux Computer and Neural Accelerator):
    • Dense stereo depth matching evaluates roughly 491 million pixel windows per second at 640×400 resolution and 30 frames per second, exceeding brain's programmable logic fabric (20,736 4-input boolean truth-table cells, called Look-Up Tables or LUT4s, and 103.5 KB of total on-chip Static RAM, or SRAM, from which brain allocates 64 KB of working memory and 16 KB of startup memory) by about three orders of magnitude.
    • Mounting sight directly behind the front lenses also keeps high-speed camera ribbon cables (Mobile Industry Processor Interface, or MIPI, Camera Serial Interface, or CSI-2, at 800 Mbps) under 4 cm long, physically isolated from 30 A motor switching currents.
    • Embedded Linux handles direct hardware camera frame transfers into memory (Direct Memory Access, or DMA) alongside an 8-bit integer Neural Processing Unit (NPU) for obstacle detection, while every geometric calibration, stereo matching, visual-inertial filtering, 3D occupancy, and path-planning algorithm is derived and implemented from first principles.

3. Home-Bench Safety and Phased Budget ​

Apex is sized to the 2.5 kg desktop quadruped class (about 35 cm body length) so assembly, bring-up, and dynamic testing stay safe inside an ordinary home workspace. Four engineering guardrails govern every physical build step:

  • Touch-Safe Voltage: Operating below 22 V DC with thermally stable LiFePO4 cells avoids shock hazards and volatile lithium-polymer pouches, and 95% of indoor bring-up runs from a current-limited bench power supply before connecting a battery.
  • Backdrivable Joints: Peak joint torque stays below 2.5 N·m so every limb moves easily by hand when compliant or unpowered and tests safely inside a tabletop slotted-aluminum gantry.
  • Zero-Fume Turnkey Fabrication: External circuit-board factories perform automated component placement and solder-paste melting (Turnkey Printed Circuit Board Assembly, or turnkey PCBA) so no solder fumes are generated indoors. Industrial parts suppliers likewise fabricate powder-fused nylon (Multi-Jet Fusion, or MJF) and precision-milled aluminum (Computer Numerical Control, or CNC) structures ready to bolt together.
  • Phased Hardware Budget: Parts average about $115 to $145 per month—roughly $290 for brain across Months 1 to 3 and about $1,495 total across all six systems over twelve months, plus a one-time $310 to $540 bench-instrument kit in Month 4 if not already on hand.

4. Verification and Hardware Bring-Up ​

Every subsystem advances through bounded, independently testable steps, with roughly 83% of milestones verified purely in simulation and automated checks before ordering or powering hardware. Three stages connect mathematical models to physical bench measurements:

  1. Simulation-First Verification: Digital logic and control laws are verified against executable software specification models (golden reference models) using pytest, the Verilator SystemVerilog simulator, and the MuJoCo physics engine. Circuit-board schematics and copper layouts pass automated Electrical and Design Rule Checks (ERC and DRC) in KiCad circuit-design software, while FPGA designs pass logic-compilation (synthesis) and timing checks in Yosys and nextpnr.
  2. Cohesive Companion Textbook: Simulation milestones grow incrementally into the 8-part, 34-chapter textbook under systems/<system>/chapters/, pairing plain intuition, interactive schematics, concrete numbers, and concise math with live-synced source excerpts.
  3. Batched Hardware Labs: Physical bench steps—turnkey board ordering, FPGA programming, copper trace pulse-echo impedance checks (Time-Domain Reflectometry, or TDR) on a handheld radio-frequency Vector Network Analyzer (VNA, LiteVNA), motor-load brake (dynamometer) torque sweeps, and untethered walking trials—append measured results as closing lab sections.
