# Apex Curriculum & Course Design Invariants

## 1. Prerequisite Closure & Course Granularity
- **100% Prerequisite Representation**: Every prerequisite topic required to understand a course or its assigned textbook chapters must be explicitly covered in an upstream prerequisite course in the DAG.
- **Bidirectional Chapter-to-Topic Alignment**: A course's assigned textbook chapter scope must both (a) cover 100% of the course's listed `Core Topics` (never omitting chapters that teach listed core topics) and (b) exclude chapters whose prerequisites are only satisfied in downstream courses.
- **Standard University Course Boundaries ("Split, Don't Cram")**: Align course scopes with standard university engineering curricula. Do not compress two distinct courses into a single hybrid course (e.g., separate Statics from Mechanics of Materials, C Systems Programming from Algorithms and Computer Architecture, Thermodynamics from Heat Transfer, Fluid Mechanics from Rotorcraft Aerodynamics, Applied Electromagnetics from Microwave RF, Mechanical Vibrations from FEA, Antennas from Digital Communications, Motion Planning from MPC, and Deep Learning from Reinforcement Learning). Only fold a topic into an existing course when it is genuinely a compact module (such as the calculus of variations within Analytical Dynamics).
- **1:1 DAG & Syllabus Synchronization**: Every course's `- **Prerequisites**:` line, course numbering, and Capstone `Unlocking Courses` entry must match the incoming edges of the Mermaid `flowchart TD` DAG in `CURRICULUM.md` and the summary tables in `README.md` 1-for-1.
- **Zero Dangling Leaf Courses (Full Capstone Convergence)**: Every course node in the curriculum DAG must have at least one outgoing edge leading directly or transitively to at least one hardware Capstone project (`C1`–`C8`). Never leave a course as an isolated dead-end leaf.

## 2. Textbook Selection Principles
- **Engineering Pedagogy Over Reference Handbooks**: Always select textbooks written for first-exposure engineering pedagogy (step-by-step derivations, physical intuition, worked examples, and graded exercises) rather than encyclopedic practitioner handbooks or pure-math/theoretical-physics monographs.
- **Single-Textbook Preference**: Prefer a single cohesive textbook that covers the entire course over stitching together multiple books. Only use two books when pairing a theory textbook with a dedicated lab/tooling guide (e.g., Circuit Theory + KiCad, OS Theory + xv6). If a course requires two theory textbooks from different sub-disciplines, evaluate whether the course should be split into two courses.
- **No Optional Textbook Clutter**: Prescribe a single authoritative reading path for each course. Never include parenthetical `(or ...)` alternative textbooks or optional reference lists.
- **Explicit Chapter Scoping & Exhaustive TOC Partitioning**: Every textbook entry must include an explicit parenthetical chapter scope verified against the edition's actual Table of Contents—either `(Ch. 1–N complete)` when the entire book is assigned, or `(Ch. X–Y; skip Ch. Z ...)` when excluding tangential/downstream chapters. The union of assigned chapters and explicitly numbered `skip Ch. ...` ranges must exhaustively partition 100% of the book's chapters `(1..N)` with zero unmentioned chapter gaps.

## 3. Declarative Single Source of Truth (`curriculum.yaml`, `progress.yaml`, `scripts/build.py` & `scripts/track.py`)
- **Canonical Data Sources**: `curriculum.yaml` is the single source of truth for all tiers, tracks, courses, prerequisites, textbook chapter partitions (`total_units`, `assigned`, `skipped`, `covered_in`, and `scope`), capstones, and home-lab specifications. `progress.yaml` is the single source of truth for incremental section-level textbook Table of Contents (`units` and `sections`) and dated section completions (`completed` and `completed_date`).
- **Automated Compilation & Verification**: Never hand-edit `CURRICULUM.md`, `PROGRESS.md`, or `README.md` without updating `curriculum.yaml` or `progress.yaml` (or running `python3 scripts/track.py`). After modifying `curriculum.yaml` or `progress.yaml`, always run `python3 scripts/build.py` to mathematically validate all DAG, textbook partitioning, and section progress invariants and regenerate `CURRICULUM.md`, `PROGRESS.md`, and `README.md` (and verify zero drift with `python3 scripts/build.py --check`).
