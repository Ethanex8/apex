# Sequential Novice Cold-Reader Rubric

Read primary lines strictly from top to bottom assuming only general programming (`if`/`else`, loops, arrays, functions, structs, byte buffers) and foundational STEM literacy—zero prior domain specialization and zero instant theorem recall. Check every bullet below against prior sections and exact diagram/code insertion points:

## 1. Layering & Forward References
1. **Zero Forward References & Foundational Coherence**: Flag any symbol, operator (`&`, `|`, `^`, `<<`, `>>`), math function, mnemonic, or theorem used before definition, and flag small sets of elementary operators scattered piecemeal downstream without one brief cohesive foundational reference.
2. **Stateless-First Ordering & Defined Mechanics**: Flag coupled multi-bit or stateful mechanisms placed ahead of simpler stateless/carry-free primitives, and flag wrap-around or hardware arithmetic invoked before its bit-level mechanism is defined.
3. **Plain Introductory Operator Summaries**: Flag introductory overview prose or table cells that cram formal range inequalities, multi-environment caveats, or symbolic domain bounds ahead of the math section; state operational behavior via simple everyday analogues and defer formal bounds to derivations.
4. **Visual Contiguity & Motivated Code Audit**: Verify schematics sit contiguously with the mechanism they clarify (never buried after proof walls) with zero premature symbols, and verify widgets and code slices follow clear intuitive Rule-of-Four explanations rather than appearing unmotivated.
5. **Concept & Mechanic Placement**: Verify no subsection motivates itself with unintroduced downstream concepts, and verify specialized mechanics sit beside their first consuming subsystem rather than in an unrelated earlier section.
6. **Abstraction Boundaries**: Flag contract-level descriptions that leak unintroduced lower-layer ports, control pins, internal signals, or unmotivated microarchitectural implementations (specific datapath topologies, stage counts, or cycle counts) before implementation sections.
7. **No Specialist Analogies or Premature Constants**: Flag analogies from niche software specialties (databases, distributed consensus, kernel paging, managed runtimes) or project-specific clock/sizing constants inside general conceptual explanations.
8. **Actor & Phase Distinction**: When offline tooling (compiler, assembler, linker) and runtime execution both touch a mechanism, name the exact actor and phase rather than umbrella nouns ("software", "system"); skip redundant toolchain recaps where behavior is purely hardware-resident.

## 2. Rule of Four, Feynman Mechanics & Reader Flow
9. **Feynman Motivation & Equal Rule of Four**: Flag sections opening with negative foils ("Before...", "Unlike...") or cold, unmotivated theorem/set-notation walls; verify every non-trivial concept builds plain mechanistic intuition (showing why naive approaches break when relevant) and chooses the clearest mix of Words, Visuals, Numbers, and Algebra.
10. **Complete Branch Coverage**: When a mechanism splits across a threshold or condition (e.g. sign-extension carry or overflow trigger), verify exact operation order and concrete values are traced explicitly across every branch without omitting edge paths; avoid duplicating non-branching linear formulas.
11. **First-Principles & Unbraided Topic Flow**: Flag non sequiturs, jumping straight to edge cases before establishing baseline behavior, unintroduced leaps, separate mechanisms braided inside one item, or multi-category table lead-ins that disproportionately elaborate one narrow parameter while neglecting major table groups.
12. **Upfront Takeaways & Honest Rationales**: Flag comparative sections that bury the main takeaway after examples, or claims boasting about avoiding language constructs (`if`/`else`) without genuine cost or correctness motivation.
13. **Balanced Economy (Zero Single-Mode Walls or Redundancy)**: Flag any mode pushed past its blind spot—tangled prose-math walls where concise algebra or tables belong, proof theater obscuring simple mechanisms, multi-step hex strings crammed in one sentence, forward section catalogs, or repeating identical rules across prose, tables, and examples.
14. **Mechanism-First Acronyms & Code-Only Backticks**: Flag detached term definitions (`— ... (**Acronym**)`), 3+ stacked new acronyms in one sentence, backticks on English acronyms/proper nouns/prose numbers, and boldface over 6 words.

## 3. Woven Examples & Hardware Payoff
15. **Woven Examples Only (Zero Standalone Example Headings or Drills)**: Flag any standalone `### Example` / `#### Example` heading, exercise (`#### Exercise`), homework section (`## <N>. Problems` / `### Problem`), or `<details>` toggle anywhere in the document; verify concrete numbers and traps live woven naturally inside section prose.
16. **Hardware-Payoff Callouts**: Whenever shared arithmetic or unified encoding eliminates duplicate circuits, mode bits, or extra multiplexers, flag explanations that pass silently over that concrete hardware payoff.
