# Sequential Novice Cold-Reader Rubric

Read primary lines strictly top to bottom as a closed-world sequential reader: treat **only** (1) general programming (`if`/`else`, loops, arrays, functions, structs, byte buffers) plus foundational STEM literacy, (2) the document's opening scope/abstraction boundary, and (3) terms already defined before the current line as known. Treat every other term, lower-layer mechanism, or deferred concept as unknown—even if listed later in the index.

## 1. Layering & Forward References
1. **Zero Forward References & Explicit Pointers**: Flag any undefined term, symbol, operator, function, or theorem used before definition, small sets of elementary primitives scattered piecemeal downstream, or vague standalone directional pointers (*"below"*, *"above"*, *"later"*) lacking an immediate adjacent element or named section.
2. **Simple-First Ordering & Defined Mechanics**: Flag coupled multi-part or stateful mechanisms placed ahead of simpler stateless primitives, and flag higher-level derived behavior invoked before its underlying primitive mechanism is defined.
3. **Plain Introductory Summaries**: Flag introductory overview prose or table cells that cram formal range inequalities, multi-environment caveats, or symbolic domain bounds ahead of the math section; state operational behavior via simple everyday analogues and defer formal bounds to derivations.
4. **Visual Contiguity & Motivated Code Audit**: Verify schematics sit contiguously with concepts (never after proof walls) with zero premature symbols, and verify widgets, helper formulas, and code slices state why callers need the produced representation—not merely what mathematical symbol it computes—before showing how it works.
5. **Concept & Mechanic Placement**: Verify no subsection motivates itself with unintroduced downstream concepts, and verify specialized mechanics sit beside their first consuming subsystem rather than in an unrelated earlier section.
6. **Declared Abstraction Boundary**: Enforce the document's opening scope boundary—flag specification, interface, or contract prose that prescribes or leaks lower-layer internal implementation machinery (even using previously defined terms) when the opening scope restricts this layer to observable behavior.
7. **No Specialist Analogies or Premature Constants**: Flag analogies from niche software specialties (databases, distributed consensus, kernel paging, managed runtimes) or project-specific sizing/tuning constants inside general conceptual explanations.
8. **Actor & Phase Distinction**: When build-time tooling and runtime execution both touch a mechanism, name the exact actor and phase rather than umbrella nouns (*"software"*, *"system"*); skip redundant tooling recaps where behavior is purely self-contained.

## 2. Rule of Four, Feynman Mechanics & Reader Flow
9. **Feynman Motivation & Equal Rule of Four**: Flag representation conversions or helper tricks that jump into avoiding a runtime constraint without first stating what fails for downstream callers on unconverted inputs, plus negative foils, cold theorem walls, or legacy catalogs—balance Words, Visuals, Numbers, and Algebra.
10. **Complete Branch Coverage**: When a mechanism splits across a threshold or condition, verify exact operation order and concrete values are traced explicitly across every branch without omitting edge paths; avoid duplicating non-branching linear formulas.
11. **First-Principles & Causal Flow**: Flag non sequiturs, unearned general claims or design slogans tacked onto a single example before the underlying invariant is established, jumping straight to edge cases before baseline behavior, braided mechanisms, or skewed table lead-ins.
12. **Upfront Takeaways & Honest Rationales**: Flag comparative sections that bury the main takeaway after examples, or claims boasting about avoiding standard language constructs (`if`/`else`) without genuine cost or correctness motivation.
13. **Balanced Economy (Zero Single-Mode Walls or Redundancy)**: Flag any mode pushed past its blind spot—tangled prose-math walls where concise algebra or tables belong, proof theater obscuring simple mechanisms, dense multi-step numeric/symbolic strings crammed in one sentence, forward section catalogs, or repeating identical rules across prose, tables, and examples.
14. **Mechanism-First Acronyms & Code-Only Backticks**: Flag detached term definitions (`— ... (**Acronym**)`), 3+ stacked new acronyms in one sentence, backticks on English acronyms/proper nouns/prose numbers, and boldface over 6 words.

## 3. Woven Examples & Contract-Level Payoff
15. **Woven Examples Only (Zero Standalone Example Headings or Drills)**: Flag any standalone `### Example` / `#### Example` heading, exercise (`#### Exercise`), homework section (`## <N>. Problems` / `### Problem`), or `<details>` toggle anywhere in the document; verify concrete numbers and traps live woven naturally inside section prose.
16. **Contract-Level Design Payoff**: Whenever a unified rule or encoding simplifies the current layer's contract or eliminates special cases, verify the prose explains that payoff concisely **at the current abstraction layer** without leaking lower-layer internal implementation details.
